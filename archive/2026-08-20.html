The Voynich Manuscript
Unknown — 1420

One herbal page of the Voynich manuscript describes a plant with blue thistle-heads, fern-ribbed leaves, and roots that flush red at the tip — a species with no match in any known flora. The text running beside it is written in an alphabet that appears in no other surviving document, and it has defeated every cryptographer and every computer that has tried it for over a century. Radiocarbon dating puts the vellum at early fifteenth-century Italy; no one has found a matching hand, a source text, or a second copy anywhere. Whoever painted the root system knew exactly what they meant it to look like — the swelling, the branching, the shading are too assured for invention on the fly. The manuscript offers no key, and none has ever turned up.

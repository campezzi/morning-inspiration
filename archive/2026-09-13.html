Fight with Cudgels
Francisco Goya — 1820

Two men are locked in a fight neither one is walking away from, sunk to the knees in what might be mud or sand, cudgels already mid-swing. Goya never named the Black Paintings; this one got its title later, from cataloguers who saw the violence and missed the trap underneath it. He put it straight onto the plaster of a house outside Madrid, deaf, alone, not long after a war that had left the countryside full of exactly this kind of anonymous brutality. No ground to retreat to, no one watching, no reason given — just the blows landing, again and again, under a flat, ashen sky.

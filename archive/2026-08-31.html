Alfa Romeo 33 Stradale
Franco Scaglione — 1967

Eighteen of these were built, their tube-frame chassis borrowed straight from Alfa's own Tipo 33 racer and beaten by hand into aluminum panels at Carrozzeria Marazzi. Nothing on the body sits still — the waistline dips and swells like a fish mid-turn, and the dihedral doors curl up into the roofline as though the whole car were caught mid-exhale. Franco Scaglione drew it in 1967; a 1995cc V8 spinning past 8,800 rpm pushed it to 260 km/h, enough to hold the standing-kilometer record the year it debuted. It's still argued over as the most beautiful car Alfa Romeo ever put a wheel under.

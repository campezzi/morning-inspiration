Reynard the Fox Preaching to Geese
Unknown London Illuminator — 1340

Below a page of dense canon law, a fox in a bishop's mitre delivers a sermon to a captive congregation of hens, a stork, and a swan, crozier gripped in one paw. It's a two-part joke: further along the same strip he's already sprinting for his den with a goose in his teeth, a woman swinging a distaff at his heels. Anonymous illuminators tucked this into the bottom margin of the Smithfield Decretals around 1340 — a page of legal minutiae with a punchline running along its baseline, ivy leaves and strawberries filling the space between panels. The same manuscript is better known for a later strip of armed, vengeful rabbits, a joke some scholars trace forward to Monty Python. Whoever drew this had strong opinions about corrupt clergy and clearly enjoyed drawing foxes more than he enjoyed the sermon above.

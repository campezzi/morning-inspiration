On Exactitude in Science
Jorge Luis Borges — 1946
In that Empire, the Art of Cartography attained such Perfection that the map of a single Province occupied the entirety of a City, and the map of the Empire, the entirety of a Province.
— Jorge Luis Borges, On Exactitude in Science (1946)

Borges published it as a single paragraph, attributed to a fictional seventeenth-century travelogue, because apparently a joke lands better with a fake citation attached. The premise: a guild of cartographers, no longer satisfied with maps that merely resemble the territory, drafts one that coincides with it point for point — an empire-sized map, unrolled across the empire itself. Later generations, less devoted to the discipline, find it useless and leave it to the weather; what survives are torn scraps in the western deserts, sheltering animals and the occasional beggar. The whole thing runs to about a hundred and thirty words in translation, which is shorter than the plaque under most maps in a museum.

Judith Slaying Holofernes
Artemisia Gentileschi — c. 1620

Judith's sleeves are rolled to the elbow, and she leans her weight into the blade like someone sawing through a stubborn bough. Her maid pins Holofernes' shoulders to the bed while he claws at them, and the blood runs in two clean arcs across the white sheet. Artemisia painted this around 1620, a few years after she testified in the trial of the man who raped her, and she gave Judith no hesitation and no halo. The light falls on forearms and steel and leaves the rest of the room in black.

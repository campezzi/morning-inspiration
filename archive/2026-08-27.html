Sunday
Edward Hopper — 1926

A man sits on the curb of an empty street in Hoboken, dressed for somewhere, going nowhere. The row of storefronts behind him — barber, tailor, whatever fills the middle building — is shut, closed on the one day he's apparently free to use them. Duncan Phillips bought the picture the year Hopper painted it and called the light "a blend of pleasure and depression," a generous way of describing a grown man with nowhere to be and no one to be there with. Hopper gives him a full block of sidewalk and a wall of blank windows and just leaves him there. Whatever day off this was supposed to be, it isn't going the way anyone pictured it.

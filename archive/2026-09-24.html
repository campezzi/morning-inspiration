Woman in Blue Reading a Letter
Johannes Vermeer — 1663

A woman stands in near-profile at a table, letter in both hands, the room cropped so tightly there's no window, no floor, no ceiling — just her and a map of Holland on the wall behind her, copied from a print by Balthasar Florisz. van Berckenrode decades earlier. It's rendered here in flat monochrome, unlike the same map showing up in color in his Officer and Laughing Girl. Her silhouette curves under the loose blue jacket in a way that's kept scholars arguing for a century — pregnant, or just the cut of the fabric. A box of pearls sits half in shadow on the table and commits to nothing. It was the first Vermeer the Rijksmuseum ever acquired, in 1885, and it still gives up the least of any of them.

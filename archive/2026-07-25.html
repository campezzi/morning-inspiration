Blood Meridian
Cormac McCarthy — 1985

It makes no difference what men think of war, said the judge. War endures.

— Cormac McCarthy, Blood Meridian (1985)

The judge says this by a fire in the middle of the book, mid-speech, and nobody around him argues back. He goes on to call war older than man, a trade waiting on its practitioner, the ultimate game because it forces a decision no other game can. McCarthy gives him the floor for pages and never once undercuts him with irony. The men listening are scalp hunters who have already done the things the judge is describing; the speech isn't persuading them of anything, just naming what they're already inside of. Forty years on, nobody's written the rebuttal.

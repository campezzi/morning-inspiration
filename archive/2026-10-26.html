Little Nemo in Slumberland: The Walking Bed
Winsor McCay — 1908

Sixteen panels, and the bed gets its legs in the fifth: Flip complains that Nemo is taking up the whole mattress, then notices the posts are growing. By the twelfth it is striding down a canyon of storefronts on stems that bend like cello necks, a big flat moon following them from panel to panel like a hole punched in the sky.

McCay draws the city with a draftsman's patience and the furniture with none at all, and the two never agree. The last panel is the only one that behaves: Nemo on the floor, tangled in the sheets, saying he was just dreaming. Nobody in the strip, or out of it, seems fully convinced.

Three Men in a Boat
Jerome K. Jerome — 1889
It always does seem to me that I am doing more work than I should do. It is not that I object to the work, mind you; I like work: it fascinates me. I can sit and look at it for hours. I love to keep it by me: the idea of getting rid of it nearly breaks my heart.
— Jerome K. Jerome, Three Men in a Boat (1889)

The confession arrives with the narrator's hands already folded, and the first sentence is the only one that sounds like a complaint. By the third he has reversed himself and is admiring the work like a painting hung in the next room. Then comes the detail that makes it: he keeps it by him, because parting with it would break his heart.

Jerome wrote it as a river-boat comedy and it has never gone out of print, which says something about how many people have recognised themselves in a man who loves a pile of labour precisely because he is not touching it.

Lancia Stratos Zero
Marcello Gandini / Bertone — 1970

The Stratos Zero stands 846 millimetres tall — about knee height on an adult — so the only way in is to lift the entire windshield like a canopy and climb down into the cockpit feet-first. Marcello Gandini built it for Bertone's stand at the 1970 Turin show, seemingly on a dare to himself: how low can a car go and still keep its steering wheel. It got a digital dashboard readout a decade before anyone else thought to try one, and a name borrowed from the stratosphere, as if regular sky wasn't ambitious enough. Eighteen years later Michael Jackson drove a bulletproofed version of it through a warehouse in Moonwalker, which is either the most or least dignified place a concept car has ever ended up.

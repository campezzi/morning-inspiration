The Day the Earth Smiled
Cassini Imaging Team, NASA/JPL-Caltech — 2013

Cassini spent about four hours tucked in Saturn's shadow, the planet blocking the sun so the spacecraft's cameras could finally look back toward it without being ruined by the glare. It gathered 141 wide-angle exposures and the team stitched them into one mosaic: the rings lit from behind like frosted glass, seven moons caught mid-orbit, and near the edge of the frame, less than a pixel wide, Earth. NASA had announced the shot ahead of time, so for fifteen minutes on July 19, 2013, people below stepped outside and looked up, some of them waving at Saturn. None of that is visible in the picture — just a pale point of light, easy to miss next to the rings unless someone has already told you where to look.

The Fight Between Carnival and Lent
Pieter Bruegel the Elder — 1559

In a Flemish town square, a fat man astride a wine barrel charges a gaunt woman on a church chair, his lance a spit of roast pork and a pig's head, her weapon a baker's paddle holding two herrings. Around them the whole town has chosen sides without much conviction: on the left, masked revellers and a man playing the bagpipes at an inn; on the right, beggars, alms and Lenten waffles outside the church.

It is the forty days before Easter staged as a bar fight, and Bruegel paints it with the straight face of a man taking minutes at the meeting. Look at the foreground, where a child spins a top and no one is watching the war.

The Fairy Feller's Master-Stroke
Richard Dadd — 1864

A woodcutter stands with his axe raised over a hazelnut, and no one in the picture moves. Around him a crowd of fairies, elves, a satyr and a dandy in a tall hat all stare at the same spot, each rendered in grass-blade detail on a ground of tangled stems, as if the whole wood were holding its breath.

Dadd painted it over nine years, mostly in Bethlem Hospital, having killed his father in 1843. Queen Mab has commissioned the stroke, to make her a new chariot, and the painting stops just before it lands. It was never finished, and the waiting has the strange effect of looking permanent.

Comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko in True Color
ESA/Rosetta/MPS for OSIRIS Team — 2014

This is the color Rosetta's camera actually saw, not the false color that usually stands in for comets: a flat, sunless grey, closer to asphalt than ice. The shape took longer to explain than the color — two lobes fused at a thin neck, like two separate worlds that drifted together and stuck. It is 4.5 billion years old and has looked more or less like this since before Earth had oceans. Rosetta orbited it for two years, close enough to watch dust lift off the neck in sunlight, and still no one is fully sure why the join hasn't come apart under its own gravity.

Apollo 12 Launch
NASA — 1969

The Saturn V is already a few hundred feet up, and the flame beneath it is longer than the rocket. It left Pad A in rain, under a ceiling of low cloud, because the launch rules were bent that morning. Thirty-six seconds in, lightning ran down the exhaust plume and into the ground; at 52 seconds it struck again, and the fuel cells dropped offline, the instruments lit up like a pinball table, and Pete Conrad radioed that they had lost everything.

A flight controller named John Aaron remembered an obscure switch, SCE to AUX, and told them to throw it. They did, and the machine kept climbing into the grey as if nothing had happened.

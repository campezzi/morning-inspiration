Cynic
Ambrose Bierce — 1911
CYNIC, n. A blackguard whose faulty vision sees things as they are, not as they ought to be. Hence the custom among the Scythians of plucking out a cynic's eyes to improve his vision.
— Ambrose Bierce, The Devil's Dictionary (1911)

Bierce built the book definition by definition in newspaper columns for two decades before it collected into a volume in 1911. This one turns the usual complaint about cynics inside out — the trouble isn't temperament, it's eyesight, too sharp for anyone's comfort, and the Scythians' solution is surgical rather than argumentative. He spent his working life as a newspaperman with a reputation for savaging bad prose in print, which may be why the joke has a journalist's economy — no wasted word, no appeal for sympathy. In 1913 he rode into Mexico to watch Pancho Villa's revolution up close and was never heard from again.

The Pig War
Unknown photographer — 1872

In June 1859 an American farmer named Lyman Cutlar found a Hudson's Bay Company pig in his potatoes and shot it. The British threatened to arrest him, and Captain George Pickett landed with 66 men to stop them; within weeks there were roughly 460 Americans ashore and five British warships in the bay. The British admiral, who had been ordered to fight, declined to start a war between two great nations over a squabble about a pig.

Both armies then camped on opposite ends of San Juan Island for twelve years, sending each other friendly invitations on holidays. This photograph is the end of it, in 1872, after Kaiser Wilhelm I sided with the Americans and the British packed up. The pig remains the only casualty.

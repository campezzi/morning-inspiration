Maman
Louise Bourgeois — 1999

Nine metres of bronze and steel on eight legs thin enough to look like they shouldn't hold it up, and the body slung overhead carrying a sac of twenty-six marble eggs. You walk straight underneath, which is the point; nobody gets to view it from a safe distance.

Bourgeois named it for her mother, a tapestry restorer who spent her days repairing what had frayed. She meant the spider as a protector, patient and clever with thread, and she was not interested in making it comfortable. Cast in 1999, it has outlived every attempt to read it as only a monster or only a tribute.

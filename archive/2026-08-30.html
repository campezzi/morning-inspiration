Leviathan
Anish Kapoor — 2011

Kapoor filled the entire nave of the Grand Palais with this — a single membrane of blood-red PVC, held taut by nothing but internal air pressure, rising three storeys and spanning nearly the whole length of the hall. Visitors entered through a blacked-out revolving door into a chamber lit only by daylight bleeding red through the skin, close enough to touch and visibly moving with the air holding it up. It ran for six weeks in 2011, marking Kapoor's return to Paris three decades after his first show there, inside one of the city's grandest nineteenth-century halls turned, for the occasion, into something closer to a lung than a room. No photograph flattens the scale back down to size.

The London Underground Map
Harry Beck — 1933

Beck was a draughtsman on the Underground's payroll who drew this in his spare time, and he threw out geography to do it: every line runs vertical, horizontal or at 45 degrees, and the stations sit at tidy intervals whether they are a hundred yards apart or two miles. The Circle is a circle, the Central line is ruler-straight, and the Thames is a gentle wobble of blue that has agreed to behave.

London's real shape is a mess, and he simply declined to show it. The company printed 500 trial copies in 1933, unsure whether anyone would want a diagram instead of a map, and it turned out everyone did. Beck kept redrawing it for decades, and was paid ten guineas for the original.

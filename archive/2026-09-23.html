Jaguar E-Type
Malcolm Sayer / Jaguar — 1961

Malcolm Sayer didn't sketch the E-Type, he calculated it — slide rule and seven-figure log tables, formulas carried over from wartime fuselage work at the Bristol Aeroplane Company, curves derived rather than drawn. He hated being called a stylist: an aerodynamicist, he said, not a hairdresser. The chassis underneath descends straight from the D-Type that won Le Mans three years running, so the shape was never really built to sit still — it's a racing car's math wearing a road car's plates. Claimed top speed, 150 mph, for a fraction of what a Ferrari cost that year. Even parked under museum light, it reads like it's mid-sentence.

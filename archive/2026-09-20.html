Hindenburg Disaster
Sam Shere — 1937

The stern is already collapsing into flame when the shutter catches it, ballast water still falling from valves that opened too late to matter. Sam Shere had two frames left in his Speed Graphic and no time to raise it to his eye — he shot from the hip, and it ran on front pages across the country by morning. Ninety-seven people were aboard when the hydrogen went up over Lakehurst; sixty-two of them got out, most of them by jumping. No zeppelin carried a paying passenger again after that evening in May.

Mama, Papa Is Wounded!
Yves Tanguy — 1927

A lone figure stands on an empty plain, smoke rising behind a shape that could be a plant or could be nothing at all — Yves Tanguy said he had the whole scene worked out in his head before he ever touched the canvas. He and André Breton liked to lift titles from books on psychiatry, phrases patients used to describe their own delusions, and this one reads like it was pulled whole from a case file. Nothing in the picture explains who mama and papa are, or why the ground has no horizon to stop it. Tanguy painted it in 1927, the year he first married, and it has hung at MoMA since 1936 without anyone settling the question.

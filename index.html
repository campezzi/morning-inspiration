Frances and the Leaping Fairy
Elsie Wright and Frances Griffiths — 1917

A girl kneels in tall grass and looks at something about the size of a hand, hovering just in front of her face. It has wings and a sharp little dress, and it is a different kind of sharp from the girl, as if the two were printed from separate negatives. Which they were: Elsie Wright, sixteen, drew the figures from a children's book, cut them out and propped them up with hatpins, then photographed her cousin Frances Griffiths beside them in the back garden at Cottingley.

Arthur Conan Doyle, who had created Sherlock Holmes, put the pictures in The Strand and believed in them. Elsie and Frances admitted the trick in 1983, though Frances kept saying the fifth photograph was real.

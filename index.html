The Space Elevator
Andrei Sokolov — 1967

Seven white spheres hang in a hexagon above the Pacific like a cluster of very large pearls, and from the lowest one a cable the width of a pencil line runs all the way down to a tiny capsule, which appears to be on its way up. Nobody in the picture seems worried about the engineering. Sokolov painted it for The Stars Are Awaiting Us, a 1967 book he made with the cosmonaut Alexei Leonov, who had walked in open space two years earlier and evidently felt entitled to speculate.

The palette is pure Soviet-optimist: lilac void, glassy blue ocean, everything lit from a sun that never sets on the project. A tower to orbit, rendered as a piece of cheerful municipal infrastructure.

SR-71B Takeoff on Afterburner
NASA / Lockheed SR-71 — 1992

Two engines at full afterburner, and the exhaust behind them is a string of bright knots, shock diamonds, each one a place where the flame is pushed back into itself. The SR-71B is only just off the runway at Edwards, nose high, a black airframe built to be a spectacle in nothing but its speed. It was designed to outrun missiles above 80,000 feet at Mach 3, and on the ground it leaked fuel until the titanium skin heated and swelled shut.

This is a 1992 NASA flight, a retired spy plane lent out to do science. It makes no effort to be gentle about any of it.

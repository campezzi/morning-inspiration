A Little Fable
Franz Kafka — c. 1920
"You only need to change your direction," said the cat, and ate it up.
— Franz Kafka, A Little Fable (c. 1920), trans. Willa and Edwin Muir

The whole story is one paragraph, and the mouse spends most of it complaining that the world is shrinking: the walls that once stood far off to either side have closed in until the trap is sitting in the last corner. The cat, who has been listening, offers the one piece of advice that is technically correct.

Kafka left it unpublished, and it surfaced in 1931, seven years after his death. The cat doesn't gloat or pounce first; it gives directions, then dinner.

First Image of a Black Hole
Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration — 2019

The ring is light bent around a hole in the universe — a black hole 6.5 billion times the mass of the sun, 55 million light-years away, in the core of the galaxy M87. Eight telescopes on four continents synchronized into one Earth-sized instrument to catch it; the data was too large for the internet, so it came back on hard drives flown by plane. What you're looking at isn't the black hole — nothing gets that — it's the shadow it throws on the matter falling into it. Released April 10, 2019, after two years of processing, it was the first time anyone had proof of what a black hole actually looks like.

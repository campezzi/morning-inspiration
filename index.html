Soldiers with Their Girls in Front of the Greyhound Bus, Indianapolis
Esther Bubley — 1943

The bus door is already open, the driver's gauges glowing inside, and nobody in the frame is looking at it. One soldier leans on the fender with a woman in a belted coat; another stands with his back to us, his hand spread across the shoulder of the woman he's holding. A leather bag sits on the concrete in the exact middle of the picture, packed and waiting.

Esther Bubley shot this in Indianapolis in 1943, working for the Office of War Information, one of hundreds of frames from her Greyhound terminals. Every shadow on the pavement is cut sharp by the canopy, a minute that has to end when the engine turns over. Down the street a man in a hat just keeps walking.

Pillars of Creation
NASA, Jeff Hester and Paul Scowen — 1995

The leftmost column runs about four light-years tip to base, gas and dust sculpted by ultraviolet light from young stars just outside the frame. Color here is information, not likeness — green marks hydrogen, red marks sulfur, blue marks oxygen, each a separate exposure from four cameras aboard Hubble, layered into one image in 1995. The same radiation that carves the pillars is eroding them, grain by grain, while denser pockets inside collapse into new stars. Astronomers once proposed a supernova tore through here six thousand years ago, though the case was never settled — the pillars sit seven thousand light-years off, so whatever's true has already happened, and word just hasn't arrived.

Woman at a Window
Caspar David Friedrich — 1822

A woman stands at an open window, seen only from behind, one hand braced on the frame. The room is bare — greenish walls, a sliver of easel at the edge — nothing to compete with the light coming off the Elbe outside. Friedrich painted his own wife this way, turned fully from the viewer, so the window becomes the only event in the picture. Two masts cross the horizon like the barest suggestion of a world going on without her. The painting holds its breath and doesn't let it out.

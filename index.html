The Tell-Tale Heart
Edgar Allan Poe — 1843
TRUE! — nervous — very, very dreadfully nervous I had been and am; but why will you say that I am mad? The disease had sharpened my senses — not destroyed — not dulled them. Above all was the sense of hearing acute.
— Edgar Allan Poe, The Tell-Tale Heart (1843)

The story opens mid-defense, before anyone has accused it of anything — a narrator insisting on his own calm while promising to tell you the whole thing, calmly. Poe sold it to James Russell Lowell's new magazine The Pioneer for ten dollars, and it ran in the first issue, January 1843. The narrator spends eight nights perfecting a silent entrance into an old man's room, then gives himself away by a sound only he can hear. The daguerreotype above was taken in the late spring of 1849, five months before Poe was found incoherent on a Baltimore street and died without ever saying why.

Rue Saint-Rustique
Eugène Atget — 1922

Rue Saint-Rustique climbs a short, steep grade in Montmartre, cobblestones catching the light and nothing else — no cars, no pedestrians, just a lamppost bracket jutting from the corner building. Atget shot it in March 1922 with a wooden view camera and glass plates that needed long exposures, which is part of why his Paris looks abandoned: anyone crossing the frame quickly enough simply failed to register on the plate. He spent three decades photographing a city he believed was disappearing, selling the prints to archives and other painters as documentation, not art. He died in 1927, a year before Berenice Abbott began buying up his negatives to keep them from being scattered. The street is still there; the particular quiet in this photograph isn't.

Judith Beheading Holofernes
Caravaggio — 1599

Judith holds Holofernes at arm's length, her face caught somewhere between effort and distaste rather than triumph, while her old servant Abra stands ready with a cloth to catch what's coming. Caravaggio sets the whole scene against flat black — no tent, no banquet table, just a red curtain and the two jets of blood already leaving the neck. He painted it around 1599, reportedly for the Genoese banker Ottavio Costa, and his Judith isn't an allegory so much as a woman doing an unpleasant job competently. The canvas dropped out of public record for centuries and turned up again only in 1950, misattributed, in a private Roman collection. Nothing in it asks to be forgiven.

Source: Galleria Nazionale d'Arte Antica, Rome — via Wikipedia

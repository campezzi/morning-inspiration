Emperor Norton Lunching with Bummer and Lazarus
Edward Jump — 1862

Norton stands at the near end of a groaning banquet table in full imperial regalia — plumed hat, epaulettes, a saber at his hip — feeding scraps to two mutts begging on their hind legs. He'd declared himself Emperor of the United States in 1859, a few years after a rice-speculation scheme wiped him out, and San Francisco simply played along: free meals, his own printed currency, decrees nobody obeyed but everybody enjoyed. Bummer and Lazarus were strays who'd earned their fame killing rats outside a Montgomery Street saloon — eighty-five in twenty minutes, once — and when a dogcatcher hauled one off in 1862, the Board of Supervisors passed a law exempting them both for good. Edward Jump, the caricaturist who kept drawing the three of them together, called this one "Three Bummers."

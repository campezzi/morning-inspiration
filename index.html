The Silent Highwayman
Unknown, Punch magazine — 1858

Punch's cartoonist drew a hooded skeleton rowing down a black, oily Thames, its caption promising to collect from everyone who hadn't paid to have the river cleaned up. That July the heat turned the sewage-choked water into something Parliament couldn't sit near — the committee room curtains were soaked in chloride of lime, and Disraeli left meetings with a handkerchief pressed to his nose, calling the Thames a "Stygian pool, reeking with ineffable and intolerable horrors." He tabled a sewer bill days later; both houses passed it within seven weeks, faster than years of health reports had managed on their own. Joseph Bazalgette got his budget and built the system still carrying London's waste today. The smell did what the reports couldn't.

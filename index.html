At Eternity's Gate
Vincent van Gogh — 1890

An old man in a wooden chair, elbows on his knees, face pushed into his palms — Van Gogh had drawn this figure once before, in 1882, a lithograph of a Hague almshouse pensioner named Adrianus Zuyderland that he titled, in his own English, "At Eternity's Gate." He returned to it in May 1890 at the asylum in Saint-Rémy, two weeks after his doctor wrote to Theo that the patient "usually sits with his head in his hands, and if someone speaks to him, it is as though it hurts him, and he gestures for them to leave him alone." He kept the old man's pose and he kept the old title. He died two months later, in July.

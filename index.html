Hubble Extreme Deep Field
NASA, ESA, G. Illingworth, D. Magee, P. Oesch, R. Bouwens and the HUDF09 and XDF Teams — 2012

Nearly every point of light in this picture is a galaxy, and a few of them are small red smudges whose light left them about 13 billion years ago. The field covers a patch of sky roughly the width of a grain of sand held at arm's length, in the constellation Fornax. Hubble stared at it for over two million seconds, spread across a decade of exposures, to collect that much faint light.

Look at the darkest corners and then at the next darkest. There is no empty part of the frame, only parts where the counting hasn't been done yet.

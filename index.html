Fulcrum
Richard Serra — 1987

Five trapezoidal plates of Cor-Ten steel, each fifty-five feet tall and three inches thick, lean against one another above the plaza outside Liverpool Street station — no welds, no bolts, just weight and friction holding the whole tower up. Serra called the engineering "axiomatic": everything holding everything else up simultaneously. Walk into the gap between the plates and you're standing inside the balance itself, looking up through a narrowing steel funnel at a slot of London sky. Nearly forty years on, commuters still cut straight through it on their way to the train.

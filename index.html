Moonrise by the Sea
Caspar David Friedrich — 1822

Three people sit on a boulder at the edge of the water, backs to us, and nobody is speaking. Two sailing ships are coming in on almost no wind, so slowly that they seem to be arriving at the same pace as the moon is rising. The figures are small and dark, the sky does nearly all the work, and the light on the water is the only thing in the picture that moves.

Friedrich painted it in 1822 as the evening half of a pair for a Berlin banker, the morning half being The Lonely Tree. It has hung in Berlin since 1861. There is time in it, and no hurry to use it.

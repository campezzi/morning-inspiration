The Lightning Field
Walter De Maria — 1977

Four hundred stainless steel poles, each about twenty feet tall, stand 220 feet apart in a grid a mile by a kilometre, in the desert of western New Mexico. The ground rises and falls, so the poles don't match in length; only their sharpened tips agree, holding one level plane over the uneven land. Lightning does strike, a few times a year, and the work doesn't care whether anyone is there to see it. Most visitors never see a strike, and De Maria meant it that way: you walk a mile of steel and wait.
